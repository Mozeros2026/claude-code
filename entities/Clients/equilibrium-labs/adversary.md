## 1. KILL-SHOT QUESTIONS
*First-two-meetings questions Equilibrium Labs can't yet answer well, ranked by severity.*

1. **"Show me the human RCT — not the mice."** The efficacy story rests on n=51 mice (Mehta 2022) and a 3-geo blood-alcohol study (Rajalingam 2017); human/clinical evidence is an open HITL item (§3, §10, §13 ⭐2). For a product making liver-"restore" claims, "preclinical" vs "clinically recommended" is the gap a hepatologist-led diligence team finds in ten minutes. **Kills the clinical-credibility thesis.**
2. **"Where does the 94.5M antioxidant number come from, and who validated ABEL-RAC?"** 94.5M units vs rosemary 819k is a ~115x outlier claim with no methodology or peer validation in the Brain (§3, §10 ASYMMETRY). A sophisticated buyer discounts it to zero on sight — and worse, it taints every other number in the deck. **Kills credibility of the headline science.**
3. **"Is the two-entity restructure actually executed, and which entity am I investing in?"** This is the Brain's single highest-leverage open question (§0, §13 ⭐1); the split is "Locked" as a decision but its execution status is lowest-confidence (§0, §8). Valuation, governance, and the entire regulatory-isolation defense all hinge on the answer. **Kills the ability to price or structure the round.**
4. **"If FDA/FTC challenges your liver-'restore' and blood-alcohol claims, what's exposed?"** Health claims draw FDA/FTC scrutiny; the two-entity split is the *mitigation*, not a cure, and substantiation files are the named asset — which the Brain doesn't confirm exist (§10 RISK, high). If the split isn't executed, the exposure isn't isolated. **Kills regulation-sensitive capital.**
5. **"What's your current cash, burn, and actual revenue in dollars?"** Brain has 189% CAGR and a $50M+/2027 forecast but no absolute revenue $, no cash, no burn (§6 FOUNDER; §0 lowest-confidence). A $3M raise can't be sized against an unknown runway. **Kills the "this round gets us there" framing.**

## 9. SUMMARY: BIGGEST EXPOSURE
The single thing that, unaddressed, kills the deal is the **collision between an aggressive health-claims narrative and a still-preclinical evidence base, while the very structure meant to contain it remains unconfirmed.** Equilibrium Labs is, unusually, a real business — 189% CAGR, live clinics, owned IP, a top-tier bench (§2, §6) — which makes the exposure all the more avoidable. But the pitch leans on a ~115x antioxidant claim with no disclosed methodology (§3, §10) and "clinically recommended" liver-restoration language supported by n=51 mice and a 3-geo study (§3), in a category where FDA/FTC scrutiny is a near-certainty (§10). The named mitigation — the two-entity restructure that isolates supplement risk into the CPG entity — is a Locked Decision whose *execution status is the Brain's single highest-leverage open question* (§0, §8, §13 ⭐1). Until that split is executed, the regulatory risk is not isolated; it sits on the consumer brand that is also our network hub. Before any high-stakes meeting, EQL must (1) confirm the restructure is done and name the raising entity, (2) produce the ABEL-RAC substantiation file or retire the headline number, and (3) state the evidence tier honestly with an RCT path in the use-of-funds. The business is strong enough that the exposure is a disclosure-and-structure problem, not a value problem — but unaddressed, it converts a fundable scale-up into an un-diligenceable one.
