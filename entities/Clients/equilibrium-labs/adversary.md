## 9. SUMMARY: BIGGEST EXPOSURE
The single thing that, unaddressed, kills the deal is the **collision between an aggressive health-claims narrative and a still-preclinical evidence base, while the very structure meant to contain it remains unconfirmed.** Equilibrium Labs is, unusually, a real business — 189% CAGR, live clinics, owned IP, a top-tier bench (§2, §6) — which makes the exposure all the more avoidable. But the pitch leans on a ~115x antioxidant claim with no disclosed methodology (§3, §10) and "clinically recommended" liver-restoration language supported by n=51 mice and a 3-geo study (§3), in a category where FDA/FTC scrutiny is a near-certainty (§10). The named mitigation — the two-entity restructure that isolates supplement risk into the CPG entity — is a Locked Decision whose *execution status is the Brain's single highest-leverage open question* (§0, §8, §13 ⭐1). Until that split is executed, the regulatory risk is not isolated; it sits on the consumer brand that is also our network hub. Before any high-stakes meeting, EQL must (1) confirm the restructure is done and name the raising entity, (2) produce the ABEL-RAC substantiation file or retire the headline number, and (3) state the evidence tier honestly with an RCT path in the use-of-funds. The business is strong enough that the exposure is a disclosure-and-structure problem, not a value problem — but unaddressed, it converts a fundable scale-up into an un-diligenceable one.
