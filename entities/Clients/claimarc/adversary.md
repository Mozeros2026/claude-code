## 1. KILL-SHOT QUESTIONS
*First-two-meetings questions ClaimArc can't yet answer well, ranked by severity.*

1. **"Where does the advance capital come from, what does it cost you, and what's your loss rate?"** — This is next-day claims financing: ClaimArc fronts cash and collects from the payor later (§1, §3). The spread between the per-claim fee and (cost-of-capital + losses) is the *entire* P&L (§6). The Brain flags capital source and loss model as the single highest-leverage open question and as a high RISK (§0, §10, §13). With no answer, there is no business to evaluate. **Kills the entire investment/lending thesis.**
2. **"You said 'if it pays lower, ClaimArc eats it' — show me the loss reserve and the underwriting model that prices that risk."** — The pitch *guarantees the hospital the upside and absorbs the downside* (§0, §1). That is a written put on payor remittance. Without a reserve and a priced underwriting model, a single bad cohort (denials, recoupments, downcoding) can wipe the spread (§6, §10). **Kills the "cheaper than factoring, risk-free to you" framing.**
3. **"Under what legal structure do you advance against healthcare claims — purchase of receivables, factoring, or lending?"** — Healthcare claim assignment and anti-assignment rules differ by payor; Medicare/Medicaid have specific reassignment restrictions (§1, §3). The Brain explicitly flags regulatory posture as an open HITL (§10). Mischaracterizing this as SaaS when it is regulated credit/factoring is a diligence landmine. **Kills licensing/compliance trust and can void the collection mechanism.**
4. **"What is your remittance-prediction accuracy at scale, on whose data, and why can't an incumbent factor replicate it?"** — The claimed moat is predicting payor payout from claims/remittance history (§3). The Brain itself marks this unconfirmed (§3 WHAT'S HARD). If accuracy is unproven, the "we price it better" edge collapses into ordinary factoring with extra balance-sheet risk. **Kills the differentiation claim.**
5. **"What is Xtract, and is it a real product or a slide?"** — The Brain lists Xtract only from Deck OS memory with no confirmed function (§3, §13 #2). Pitching a two-product company where one product is undefined invites the question "what else is aspirational?" **Kills credibility of the product narrative.**
