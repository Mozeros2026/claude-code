**Overall: 2.8/5 — TRACK → conditional lean-in.**
A genuinely differentiated, distribution-led play with real, ownable assets and
a signed revenue contract — rare at seed. But it is **not a clean institutional
deal**: it carries a reputational gate, a newly-public counterparty, a
related-party transaction, and a fantasy conversion rate. For thesis-aligned
capital (e.g. loveyourliver.com) it merits real diligence; for conversion-
sensitive or brand-sensitive capital it is likely a pass.

## The four gates (must clear before any term sheet)

1. **Reputational appetite.** Del Bigtree / Tracy Beanz are the audience *and*
   the risk (advertiser flight, deplatforming, payment-processor and health-
   claim regulatory exposure). Decide if this is thesis or disqualifier — it's
   the central go/no-go.
2. **RTB counterparty.** RTB only listed 2026-05-13 via a RYVYL reverse merger.
   The guaranteed floor and ~⅓ of media revenue depend on it. Verify financial
   stability, contract enforceability, and the 450M-user claim independently.
3. **Related-party portal acquisition.** $1M of investor cash → founder-
   affiliated nonprofit (asset built with a $1.3M grant), at a self-assessed
   "fair market value." Demands independent valuation + governance review.
4. **Unit economics.** The 60% freemium conversion is the load-bearing
   assumption. Re-underwrite the whole model at 5–10% and see if it still works.

## What's strong (don't lose sight of)

- A real, ownable distribution advantage most seed companies lack.
- A signed, already-paying media contract with a Nasdaq-listed partner.
- Owns the core commercial asset (directory IP).
- Large, fast-growing, tailwind-rich market with credible bottom-up sizing.

Terms arrive · RTB diligence completes · the model is re-underwritten at a
realistic conversion rate · the directory actually launches with early metrics.
